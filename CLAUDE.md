# caffeine-forecast

Forecasting US caffeine consumption by generation using 25 years of CDC NHANES data.

## Purpose

Portfolio project for business analyst roles. Scoped deliberately to **predictive
analytics only** — descriptive and prescriptive work belongs in separate projects.

Framing is senior-BA / CPG strategy: the audience is a beverage category team, and
outputs must translate into demand and risk implications, not just health stats.

## Working style

The user is learning, not outsourcing. **Guide rather than write code directly into
files.** Give code blocks in chat with short explanations of what each part does,
plus markdown notes she can paste into notebooks so a reader understands the work.
Keep explanations short. Code should be concise and idiomatic (method chaining,
`.query()`, `.assign()`), at the level of an analyst with a couple of years'
experience.

Constraints: everything must be free (no paid tools or panels). She works on a Mac,
2-3 hours a day. Power BI Desktop is Windows-only, so the dashboard is Tableau Public.

## Data notes

- 11 NHANES cycles, 1999-2000 through 2021-2023. There is no K cycle (COVID).
- File naming: `DEMO_<suffix>` and `DR1TOT_<suffix>`. Pre-2003 cycles use `DRXTOT`
  instead, because two-day dietary recall started in 2003. 1999-2000 has no suffix.
- Caffeine variable: `DRXTCAFF` before 2003, `DR1TCAFF` from 2003.
- Weight: `WTDRD1` (day-one **dietary** weight) in every cycle. Do NOT use
  `WTMEC2YR` — dietary analyses require the dietary weight.
- SAS xport stores zero as ~5.4e-79; snap to 0.
- All statistics must be survey-weighted. Unweighted 2017-18 mean is 141 mg;
  weighted is 166.6 mg, against a published benchmark of 169 mg.
- Pooled analytical sample: 57,459 adults 18+.
- Columns are renamed at load to plain English: person_id, age, sex, income_ratio,
  caffeine_mg, diet_weight, cycle, cycle_start, birth_year, generation.

## Findings so far

- **Each later birth year consumes ~3.25 mg/day less caffeine at the same age**
  (survey-weighted, R² 0.62). Unweighted estimate agrees at -3.15.
- Robust across life stages: slopes negative in the 20s, 30s, 40s and 50s.
  The 20s band covers births to 2000, so early Gen Z shows the same decline.
- Intake rises with age within every cohort, peaking around 45-50.
- Heavy users (>400 mg/day, the FDA limit) halve each generation:
  Boomers peaked at 21%, Gen X ~10% and flat, Millennials ~5%, Gen Z under 1%.
  Millennials show no sign of rising toward Gen X levels as they age.
- **The original hypothesis (each generation consumes more) is contradicted.**
  The project was reframed around structural category decline instead.

## Known limitations to keep stating

- Association, not causation. Cross-sectional survey data.
- Single-day 24-hour recall; day-2 recall exists from 2003 and is unused.
- Birth year is approximate (age at screening, cycles span two years).
- Cohorts born after ~2005 are not observed, so 2035 projections extrapolate
  beyond the fitted range.
- Pooling cycles into a single estimate would require dividing weights by the
  number of cycles; current analysis is within-cycle so this does not apply.
- 2021-2023 spans three years, not two.

## Conventions

- Every notebook has its own import cell; nothing carries between them.
- Aggregations are weighted via sum-product, not `.apply()`.
- Cells with fewer than 50 observations are excluded from plots.
- Commit messages describe what the commit does, present tense.
- Raw data is never committed; the ingestion script regenerates it.
